# The paper shelf: numbers, labels and separator sheets

## Number the paper you keep

Turn on **Settings → Paper shelf → Number the paper I keep**. Uploading and scanning then ask "Keeping the
paper?" and offer the next free number. Write it on the paper (or stick a label on it) and file the paper in
number order: a box or a folder per hundred works well. Type `#124` in the Library search or in Ask to find
the document for a sheet, and every document's page shows its number.

## Labels

In the **Library**, select documents and tap **Print labels** for Avery 5160 (Letter), L7160-style (A4)
sheets or single 62 mm labels. Each label has the number, the title, the date and a QR code that opens the
document.

**Blank labels, ahead of time:** print a sheet of numbers before the paper exists and stick one on each
sheet as you file it. Printed numbers are reserved, so uploads never reuse them. When a scanned document has
one of these labels on its first page, PaperJay reads it and gives the document that number by itself.

## Scan a whole stack at once: separator sheets

Print a separator sheet (**Settings → Paper shelf**, or **Print labels** in the Library; Letter or A4) and
put one between documents. Scan the whole stack as one PDF (a sheet feeder, a scanning app, or the phone
camera) and upload it, or drop it in the consume folder. PaperJay makes one document of each stretch of pages
and leaves the separator pages out; the scanned stack goes to the Trash (you can restore it for 30 days).
Tags, company and type given at upload go to every part, and a paper number claimed at upload goes to the
first part. Reuse the sheets as often as you like.

Both the labels and the separator sheets are read from PDFs of up to 200 pages. Turn it off with **Read
separator sheets and labels when scanning** in Settings → Paper shelf.

## When the paper can go

See [Keeping and shredding paper](keeping-paper.md): PaperJay tells you which numbered papers you've kept
long enough.
